# Michael Hedges, Ph.D.

**Associate Scientist · Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory**

I work on the physics and engineering of intense particle beams, from production targets to the instrumentation needed to understand and monitor them. At Fermilab, I lead development of the Mu2e production target and work on instrumentation for target-health monitoring and beam diagnostics. I am also the **Target Group Leader for the US Muon Collider Collaboration** ([USMCC organization](https://muoncollider.us/contact)).

## Research

My research connects precision particle physics with the accelerator systems, targets, and instrumentation that make demanding experiments possible. I am particularly interested in experiments involving muons, where precision measurements and searches for rare processes offer powerful ways to test the Standard Model and look for new physics.

### Muons, precision physics, and future facilities

At Fermilab, I lead development of the Mu2e production target and work on instrumentation for target-health monitoring and beam diagnostics. Mu2e searches for charged-lepton flavor violation through the conversion of a muon into an electron, probing physics beyond the Standard Model.

I'm also interested in the broader future of muon-based experiments, from Mu2e-II and proposed advanced muon facilities to the longer-term prospect of a muon collider. These facilities pursue different scientific goals and have distinct technical requirements, but all raise important questions about how to produce, transport, measure, and use intense particle beams. I've contributed to discussions of future muon facilities through invited and contributed talks, and I see targetry and instrumentation as important parts of enabling their scientific reach.

### High-power targetry and beam instrumentation

High-power targetry is inherently multidisciplinary. Particle production, heat transfer, radiation damage, materials behavior, mechanical integrity, and instrumentation are coupled problems, and progress depends on bringing together expertise from across these fields.

My current work focuses on the Mu2e production target and the instrumentation needed to understand and monitor target and beam conditions. I'm interested in how these capabilities can inform the development of future high-intensity facilities, while recognizing that each facility will bring its own engineering challenges.

### Detector development and precision measurements

My earlier work with Belle II included angular analyses of semileptonic B-meson decays and tests of lepton-flavor universality. My detector research has also included directional neutron detection, tracking detectors, and novel scintillator technologies, including quantum-dot-based detectors.

These projects reflect complementary approaches to experimental particle physics: precision measurements of known processes, searches for rare or forbidden phenomena, and the development of new instrumentation to extend what experiments can measure.

## Collaboration

High-power targetry is inherently multidisciplinary. Progress depends on bringing together people who understand different parts of the problem—from accelerator physics and particle production to materials, simulation, engineering, and instrumentation.

I enjoy working with curious, motivated people who want to tackle difficult problems that cross disciplinary boundaries. There are important questions to solve both for experiments being built today and for facilities still taking shape. If your expertise connects with these challenges, I'd be glad to talk.
